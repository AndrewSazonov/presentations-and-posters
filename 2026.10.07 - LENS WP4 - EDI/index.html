What modern analysis needs

Large series · up to 1000 datasets × 100 patterns each temperature, field and time series from one experiment

Multidimensional data and very large parameter spaces 2D and 3D detectors, joint refinement of many patterns at once

Automation · experiments that run themselves sequencing and recovery without a human · a bad dataset must not stop the run

As the data grows, performance matters more and more

Source: RWG_requirements.pdf (physics literature), transcribed in crysta docs/dev/requirements/rwg-requirements.md. Sections 01 "Support large sequential datasets (~1000) and many (50–100) histograms per point", 02 "Handle very large parameter spaces in sequential workflows" and "Define realistic performance targets for high-throughput use", 04 "Use data-quality signals to guide autonomous or semi-autonomous experiments", 10 "Leave room for cloud, GPU, or supercomputer-backed execution". Also in the brief: neutron and X-ray, monochromatic and TOF, magnetic and modulated models, scriptable APIs, plugins, LLM support.

The experiment

How far can we get if AI writes every line
of a real crystallographic engine?

The experiment covers the new engine code, command line, bindings, tests and build setup. Existing libraries remain essential human contributions. This is work towards a usable product, with broader validation still needed.

The test case

Sample · NCAF, Na2Ca3Al2F14 a standard reference powder · cubic I213, a = 10.2503 Å, six sites

Measurement · WISH at ISIS, banks 2 + 9 merged one pattern at 2θ = 58.33° · 3977 points, 627 peaks in the fitted range

Refinement · 48 free parameters coordinates, displacement, scale, zero, profile, background · same start for every engine

Reference · FullProf CLI 8.40 fp2k · same model and instrument parameters · every difference is measured against it

Machine · VISA, an Intel Xeon node · 6 cores runs use 1 or 6 threads, as labelled

Everything in this section is one case: the WISH banks 2 and 9 time-of-flight pattern of the NCAF standard, refined from one declared start with the same 48 free parameters in every engine, on a VISA node (the facility’s remote analysis service; Intel Xeon Cascadelake with AVX-512, 32 GB, Ubuntu 22.04). The box has 64 cores, but every figure in this talk uses 1 or 6 threads — the 64-thread rows are in the backup. FullProf is the reference. Say this once here and the shorthand on the charts is readable.

329 s / 838 patterns
FullProf fp2k (Fortran)
13 s / 16 cycles
crysta estimate (C++20)
3.2 s / 37 equivalents
crysta’s bar is its measured single-thread refinement time multiplied by three, so the comparison uses a slower descent strategy rather than the fastest one. The committed descent comparison (enhantica/bench) puts the alternatives at 3.2× and 6.0× the default ladder at one thread; three is the round figure inside that range. The other bars: CrysPy through EasyDiffraction 468 s, CrysPy direct 329 s, FullProf 13 s. Every bar is one thread.
