Everything in this section is one case: the WISH banks 2 and 9 time-of-flight pattern of the NCAF standard, refined from one declared start with the same 48 free parameters in every engine, on a VISA node (the facility’s remote analysis service; Intel Xeon Cascadelake with AVX-512, 32 GB, Ubuntu 22.04). The box has 64 cores, but every figure in this talk uses 1 or 6 threads — the 64-thread rows are in the backup. FullProf is the reference. Say this once here and the shorthand on the charts is readable.

Recomputing reflections that symmetry makes identical

Numbers: enhantica/bench, calculator/results/calc.md — the run generated 2026-09-05 16:14 UTC for every bar and for crysta after the fix (1 thread 29 ms, 6 threads 6 ms); the two before-fix crysta bars are that same harness’s last pre-fix run (bench 3f9ff81: 584 ms and 119 ms). Cross-check, relay C11-T50, one harness, single thread, at this case’s cutoff of 12.25 FWHM: 638.0 ms before the fold (crysta b34b818a) against 29.4 ms after (c89e6b23), 21.7×. The corpus’s 7,394 signed reflections decompose into 357 space-group and Friedel orbits whose members share d and |F|², so every member carries the same contribution; the 20.7× reflection-count ratio is what the speed-up tracks. The answer was never wrong — the work was. The fold is model-conditional: preferred orientation, magnetic scattering or a non-symmetry-adapted width each switch it off.

The fits have similar, but different, final values: reduced χ² 4.743 for lmfit, 4.871 for crysta. Be precise if asked: a crysta LM step is NOT one calculation. Measured single-threaded, the value pass is 29 ms and the analytic Jacobian 59 ms, about two value passes for all 48 derivatives; a whole iteration is six, the rest being trial steps in the damping ladder. So six steps cost about 36 calculations, 37 counting the starting evaluation, against 643. FullProf is the most step-efficient engine here, about 10 calculations for its 16 cycles, because it also uses analytic derivatives.
