Excellent science, each with its own interface.

Neutron and X-ray powder diffraction alone: a dozen programs in daily use and as many libraries under them. All scientifically excellent, most written over decades by small teams. A user who needs two of them learns two interfaces, two input formats and two sets of conventions.

Gaps for the ESS instruments, and a steep start for new users.

First what is missing: site susceptibility, needed for MAGIC, exists only in CrysPy and CCSL; 2D Rietveld, needed for DREAM, only in a modified GSAS-II. Then the row slides on to what makes a start hard: a busy main window, input files with terse labels, extra software to install, a program for one platform only. The science in all of them is excellent; say so.
