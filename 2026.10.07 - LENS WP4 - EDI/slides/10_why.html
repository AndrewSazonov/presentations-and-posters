Excellent science — every tool with its own interface, files and conventions

Neutron and X-ray powder diffraction alone: a dozen programs in daily use and as many libraries underneath them. All of them scientifically excellent, most written over decades by very small teams. A user who needs two of them learns two interfaces, two input formats and two sets of conventions.

Hard to start, and missing what new instruments need

The first three are about getting started: everything on screen at once, input files written for the program rather than for people, and extra software to install before anything runs. Then the row slides on, and the problems stop being about comfort: Jana runs on Windows only, site susceptibility for MAGIC exists only in CrysPy and CCSL, and 2D Rietveld for DREAM only in a modified GSAS-II. Say again that the science in all of these is excellent.
